1.  The entropy-barrier multilayer layer does not pay off.  Plain LS with
    wider windows dominates it at every matched memorization level, on both
    metrics.  Layer 2's weights sum to 1, so it *replaces* layer 1's estimate
    rather than refining it.  (hshunt is re-deriving the layer-2 posterior.)

2.  The result came from the control: the per-step window schedule, not the
    layer count, moves the metrics.  Uniform x2 takes CIFAR-10 FID 189 -> 94,
    CelebA 128 -> 79, MNIST 19.0 -> 17.0, with zero training-set copies.
    (IS and FID disagreed three times here; FID won each time.)

3.  And the schedule's *shape* beats its width.  Ramping the multiplier down
    from 3.4 at t=0 to 1.6 at t=1 beats uniform x2 at matched memorization
    and zero copies -- CIFAR-10 FID 83/86 vs 94/93 over two seeds, CelebA
    70 vs 79 -- and is still the operating point.

4.  Six controls now bound the shape mechanism and all fail: up-ramp, flat,
    pushing either end alone, and both axes of the (level, compression)
    parameterization.  Each trades memorization for quality along a single
    frontier; none moves the frontier itself.  The robust gap is between
    shape *classes* -- compressed and tilted sit 7-10 FID below uniform and
    flat at every matched distance, in two independent parameterizations.

5.  Step count is the first knob that leaves that frontier.  Every run until
    today took 20 steps, because the sampler takes one per window entry.
    Resampling the champion schedule onto 60 steps -- same window at every
    noise level, finer integration -- gives CIFAR-10 FID 83.3 -> 78.8 and
    IS 6.44 -> 6.75 while the median distance to the training set *grows*.
    Caveat: IS is non-monotone, +0.31 is one split-spread, and the copy tail
    rises 0.2% -> 1.6%.  A seed replicate is running; FID is the solid part.

6.  Next: re-tune the window schedule at the better step count, then stop
    fitting shapes.  The ImageNet blocker is not the candidate search, which
    is already O(1) in image size, but the full pass over the training set at
    every step -- and more steps multiply exactly that cost.
