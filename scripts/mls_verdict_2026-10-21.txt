1.  The multilayer layer does not pay for itself.
    Both the entropy-barrier MLS and plain LS climb toward the real-image Inception
    Score as they drift toward the training set. At every matched nearest-neighbour
    distance, plain LS is the higher of the two, e.g.

        EBMLS barrier 0.25 : IS 7.04, 19.6% of samples are copies
        LS windows x2.5    : IS 7.78, 14.6% of samples are copies

    So the second layer buys Inception Score only by memorizing, and buys it less
    efficiently than simply widening the single-layer window. As specified, layer 2
    replaces rather than refines: its weights sum to 1 at each pixel, so
    x_hat_1 + delta is exactly the layer-2 posterior mean over training images, with
    layer 1 entering only through the weights. That is what makes it collapse onto
    training images as soon as the window is allowed to grow.

2.  Window width is where the Inception Score actually was.
    The scales file is far from the best operating point for this metric. Scaling
    every per-step window by 2 takes CIFAR-10 from IS 2.78 to 6.29 with no sample
    within the copy threshold of a training image. Past that it stops being free:
    x2.5 copies 15% of samples and x3 copies 51% while reaching IS 8.40, which is
    essentially the real-image score of 8.75 and means nothing.

3.  The barrier does discover a sensible schedule.
    Nothing imposes one, yet the selected layer-2 window runs from the full image at
    high noise down to zero (layer 2 off, pure LS) at low noise. The coarse-to-fine
    ordering is real; it is the layer-2 update rule that is not worth its cost.

4.  Open question for the ImageNet goal.
    Candidate windows already cost the same at any resolution (10 candidates at 32x32
    and at 256x256, via integral-image box sums). The blocker is the per-step pass
    over the whole training set, which still scales with N and will need a candidate
    subset or approximate nearest-neighbour search before full-resolution ImageNet.
