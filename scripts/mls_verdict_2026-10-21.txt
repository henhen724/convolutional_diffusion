1.  The entropy-barrier multilayer layer does not pay off.  It is dominated by
    plain LS with wider windows at every matched memorization level, on both
    metrics.  FID sharpens this: barrier 1 beats the LS baseline on IS
    (3.30 vs 2.78) but is clearly worse on FID (201 vs 189), so its apparent
    IS gain was not a distribution gain.

    Why: layer 2's per-pixel weights sum to 1, so x1 + d2 = sum_n w_n(p) x0_n(p)
    exactly.  Layer 2 replaces layer 1's estimate rather than refining it, and
    widening its window walks straight into copying.  (The layer-2 posterior is
    under revision -- hshunt is re-deriving the correct posterior for a
    training-set image -- which will change these numbers but not this point.)

2.  The result came from the control: the per-step window schedule, not the
    layer count, is what moves the metrics.  Uniformly scaling the windows x2
    improves every dataset with zero training-set copies:
      CIFAR-10   IS 2.78 -> 6.29,  FID 189 -> 94
      CelebA     IS 2.39 -> 2.34,  FID 128 -> 79
      MNIST      IS 1.86 -> 1.92,  FID 19.0 -> 17.0
    Past x2 the gains are bought by copying (x2.5 copies 15%, x3 copies 51%).

3.  IS alone would have read CelebA as "no effect" (flat at ~2.4): an ImageNet
    classifier has nothing to say about faces, and real CelebA images only
    score 3.31.  FID is what shows the window-width win generalizes.

4.  The widening must be coherent across the trajectory.  x2 on only half the
    schedule gives IS 3.06 (high-noise half) or 4.00 (low-noise half) against
    2.78 baseline; both halves together give 6.29 -- strongly super-additive.

5.  But coherent is not the same as uniform.  Ramping the multiplier down from
    3.4 at t=0 to 1.6 at t=1 -- loading the extra width onto the low-noise end
    and flattening the base schedule -- beats uniform x2 at matched
    memorization, on two datasets and two seeds:
      CIFAR-10  NN-RMS 0.267/0.271 vs 0.270/0.274, FID 83.3/86.1 vs 94.0/93.0
      CelebA    NN-RMS 0.301      vs 0.302,        FID 70.0      vs 79.4
      MNIST     no effect (on the uniform frontier, and uninformative anyway)
    The up-ramp is the control and it fails: 1.4->2.6 gives FID 89.8 at NN-RMS
    0.227, worse than the uniform curve at the same point (~77).  So the tilt's
    direction matters, and it runs against the coarse-to-fine intuition: the
    base schedule is already too steep and the fine-detail steps are the ones
    starved of context.

6.  Next: fit the per-step schedule directly against FID under a zero-copy
    constraint, rather than searching two-parameter ramps.  For ImageNet, the
    candidate search is already O(1) in image size via integral images; the
    remaining blocker is the full pass over the training set at every step.
