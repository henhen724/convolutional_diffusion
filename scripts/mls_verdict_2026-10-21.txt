1.  The entropy-barrier multilayer layer does not pay off.  On CIFAR-10 it is
    dominated by plain LS with wider windows at every matched memorization
    level, on both metrics.  FID sharpens this: barrier 1 beats the LS
    baseline on IS (3.30 vs 2.78) but is clearly worse on FID (201 vs 189),
    so its apparent IS gain was not a distribution gain.

2.  Why: layer 2's per-pixel weights sum to 1, so x1 + d2 = sum_n w_n(p) x0_n(p)
    exactly.  Layer 2 replaces layer 1's estimate rather than refining it, and
    widening its window walks straight into copying training images.
    (The layer-2 posterior itself is under revision — hshunt is re-deriving
    the correct posterior for a training-set image, which will change these
    numbers but not the structural point.)

3.  The real result came from the control.  Uniformly scaling the per-step LS
    windows x2 improves every dataset with zero training-set copies:
      CIFAR-10   IS 2.78 -> 6.29,  FID 189 -> 94
      CelebA     IS 2.39 -> 2.34,  FID 128 -> 79
      MNIST      IS 1.86 -> 1.92,  FID 19.0 -> 17.0
    Reproduced across two seeds on CIFAR-10.  Past x2 the gains are bought by
    copying: x2.5 copies 15% of samples, x3 copies 51%.

4.  IS alone would have read CelebA as "no effect" (flat at ~2.4), because an
    ImageNet classifier has nothing to say about faces -- real CelebA images
    only score 3.31.  FID is what shows the window-width win generalizes.

5.  The widening has to be coherent across the whole trajectory.  Applying x2
    to only half the schedule gives IS 3.06 (high-noise half) or 4.00
    (low-noise half) against 2.78 baseline; both halves together give 6.29.
    +0.28 and +1.22 separately, +3.51 jointly -- strongly super-additive.

6.  Next: the per-step window schedule is the thing worth optimizing, not the
    layer count.  EBMLS's candidate search is already O(1) in image size via
    integral images; the remaining blocker for ImageNet is the full pass over
    the training set at every step.
