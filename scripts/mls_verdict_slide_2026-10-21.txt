1.  The entropy-barrier multilayer layer does not pay off.  It is dominated by
    plain LS with wider windows at every matched memorization level, on both
    metrics.  Layer 2's weights sum to 1, so it *replaces* layer 1's estimate
    rather than refining it.  (hshunt is re-deriving the layer-2 posterior.)

2.  The result came from the control: the per-step window schedule, not the
    layer count, moves the metrics.  Uniform x2 takes CIFAR-10 FID 189 -> 94,
    CelebA 128 -> 79, MNIST 19.0 -> 17.0, all with zero training-set copies.

3.  And the schedule's *shape* beats its width.  Ramping the multiplier down
    from 3.4 at t=0 to 1.6 at t=1 beats uniform x2 at matched memorization and
    zero copies: CIFAR-10 FID 83/86 vs 94/93 over two seeds, CelebA 70 vs 79.
    This is the current operating point.

4.  Three controls bound the mechanism, and all three fail.  Up-ramp: worse
    than uniform.  Flat schedule: no better, worse at the zero-copy point.
    Pushing either end alone: still beats uniform, but loses to the balanced
    ramp.  The schedule behaves as one object -- the ends move together.

5.  Compressing the schedule's dynamic range is a second, FID-only win:
    FID 102 at NN-RMS 0.289 against ~121 for uniform at the same distance,
    while IS reads it as a loss.  IS and FID disagreed three times here; FID
    is the one to trust, since IS has almost no range on CelebA or MNIST.

6.  Next: fit the per-step schedule directly against FID under a zero-copy
    constraint.  For ImageNet the candidate search is already O(1) in image
    size; the blocker is the full pass over the training set at every step.
